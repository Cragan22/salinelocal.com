Equinovate Therapy Services

Bauxite, AR · Health & Wellness

About Equinovate Therapy Services

Sara Kirchner graduated from Bauxite High School in 2010 and knew right then that small-town life was what she wanted. Life took her away for a few years, but she bought a house back in Bauxite so her kids could grow up where she did. Today she runs Equinovate Therapy Services, a pediatric therapy company built on faith, family, and a few horses who've been through some things themselves.

Afraid of Horses, Until Her Son Found His Words

Sara will tell you she was terrified of horses growing up. One threw her when she was young, and she wanted nothing to do with them after that. Then a friend with a horse for sale invited her family out, and they started taking lessons on it. Her youngest son, who has apraxia of speech, began giving the horse commands like "go" and "stop." Watching him communicate from the saddle changed everything for her. Sara went through hippotherapy certification and got fully trained, and she still calls her son the reason she does this work.

Families, Not Numbers

Before Equinovate, Sara spent more than ten years in outpatient pediatrics with another company, and over time she felt like patients were being treated as numbers. So about three years ago, she and occupational therapist Brandi Simpson started their own practice. Sara kept her old job while she built it, then took what she calls the biggest, scariest jump of her life about a year and a half ago and went full time. "God always provides," she says.

What started as an occupational therapy practice now offers speech therapy too, plus physical therapy through a partnership with Rock Town Therapy. The team has grown to 12 people serving around 191 kids, and Sara's dad even pitches in part time as office manager. Her rule hasn't changed through any of that growth. The team knows every family, down to the siblings and what mom does for a living. Sara describes the company as built on faith, trust, and family, and she wants the way it treats people to reflect Christ.

Horses With a Story

Equinovate's hippotherapy horses aren't perfect, and that's on purpose. Jackson, the hat thief of the bunch, came from a shelter and has a scar on his leg. Another horse ran through a fence before the family got him and still carries scars across his back. For the toddlers, there's Bubbles, a mini pony.

Sara believes a polished show horse can feel intimidating, and that kids open up to an animal that's been through something. "We want all of our horses to have a story, because all of our kids have a story," she says.

One teenager in foster care showed up to her first session so upset she refused to get on the horse. She earned her time with the horses session by session, and now she rides all over the property. Even after moving to a new foster home, she still comes back to visit them.

Sara leads about 12 hippotherapy sessions a week at the farm in Bauxite, and there's currently a waitlist.

Meeting Kids Where They Are

When they're not at the farm, Equinovate's therapists are on the road, working with kids in schools, daycares, and homes. Sara recently completed her certification as an autism specialist, and working with kids on the spectrum is her favorite part of the job.

A lot of what the team does is sensory integration, and Sara explains it simply. Some kids are seekers who can't stop moving. Others are avoiders who barely engage. Neither one is a behavior problem. They just need to be met where they are. For a seeker, she compares it to getting a big, deep hug when you're upset and feeling your whole body relax. Weighted blankets, weighted vests, and the steady rhythm of a horse all help settle things down. For an avoider, the team does the opposite and gets them spinning and moving.

Feeding therapy is personal for Sara too. Her oldest son needed it as a baby, and Equinovate now has two therapists fully trained in it. One recent client couldn't stand to touch noodles, so Sara built a sensory bin with shaving cream, noodles, and little toy dinosaurs to help him get comfortable with the texture.

No Child Turned Away

Sara never wants a child to go without therapy because of money. Equinovate sponsors a few kids whose care insurance won't cover, and its therapists can donate part of their paychecks to a fund for families. That fund has helped a family with bills and bought new school shoes for several kids this year.

The giving reaches well past their own clients. Sara recently filled more than 90 blessing boxes around the community with goody bags that include a ramen meal pack, and the team brings more to every vendor event they work. Their table usually has a simple message for anyone who needs one: take one.

A Home Base Opening in December

Right now Equinovate is mostly mobile, and Sara says that has sometimes cost them families who wanted a place to come to. That changes this December, when their new clinic opens on Kenwood Road in Benton, near the Tinseltown theater. Sara is planning a Christmas grand opening with a ribbon cutting and a visit from Santa.

Still Showing Up

Sara is running all of this while going through cancer treatment. At first she didn't want anyone to know. Then she decided it's simply part of her story. The team is still working, still growing, and still showing up for the families who count on them.
